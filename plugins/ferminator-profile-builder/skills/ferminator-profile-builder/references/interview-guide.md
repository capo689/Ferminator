# Guided onboarding interview

Ask no more than five questions per round. Summarize confirmed facts,
uncertainties, and decisions before continuing.

## Round 1 — Identity, materials, and history

1. What name should Ferminator display?
2. Attach a résumé or equivalent career history.
3. Which LinkedIn, portfolio, GitHub, personal-site, or project URLs are yours?
4. May public professional sources be searched and shown for confirmation?
5. Can you provide a prior-application ledger, and should matches recur after
   six months at the job level or company level?

## Round 2 — Evidence and credibility

Ask only what source review does not establish:

- What work did the user personally own versus support?
- What changed because of it, and which metrics are safe to use?
- What were the team, budget, client, and executive scopes?
- Which tools and domains were used directly?
- Which qualifications, titles, credentials, or scale must not be claimed?

Distinguish ability to perform the work from evidence likely to survive ATS and
recruiter screening.

## Round 3 — Role-family decisions

- Which work is core, adjacent, edge, or deliberately exploratory?
- What must each real match actually involve?
- What titles commonly mean the wrong work?
- Which signals rescue an ambiguous title?
- Which central responsibilities or qualification gaps make the role Wrong?

Draft role families with evidence, false positives, tolerated gaps, and
non-claims. Present them for correction without asking the user to invent
numeric thresholds.

## Round 4 — Great, Maybe, and Wrong

- What combination makes an opportunity an immediate Great?
- What concerns turn a good opportunity into Maybe?
- Which conditions are absolute Wrong decisions?
- Which gaps reduce callback probability but do not defeat functional fit?
- Which work patterns are undesirable: quota, travel, administration,
  internal communications, management, agency pace, consulting delivery,
  on-call work, hands-off strategy, or deep engineering?

Convert answers into structured eligibility and desirability rules.

## Round 5 — Geography and economics

- Confirm ZIP, radius, remote countries/regions, timezone, hybrid frequency,
  relocation, local markets, and travel ceiling.
- Confirm minimum base, target base, exceptional-opportunity floor, hourly
  floor, and acceptable bonus/equity tradeoffs.
- Decide whether missing compensation is eligible for review.
- Record geographic or compensation exceptions explicitly.
- Confirm full-time, contract, contract-to-hire, or other employment types.

## Round 6 — Companies, delivery, and beta operations

- Classify company industries, stages, and sizes as prefer, accept, avoid, or
  never show.
- Choose dashboard and optional email delivery.
- Record a preferred scan time; explain that the beta administrator assigns
  the actual run slot.
- Confirm the default freshness policy or document a justified exception.
- Review unresolved conflicts and conservative choices.

## Calibration run

After the profile validates:

1. Run an intentionally broad initial search.
2. Present 20–40 diverse real jobs when supply permits; this is a calibration
   batch, not a permanent quota.
3. Capture Great reason, Maybe tradeoff, Wrong reason code and explanation,
   and Duplicate.
4. Recalibrate retrieval, eligibility, and Great-versus-Maybe ranking.
5. Record applied, interviewed, rejected, and offer outcomes separately so
   callback probability can improve without redefining personal desirability.

Do not call onboarding calibrated before the real-job review step. If the user
cannot complete it immediately, deliver a structurally valid provisional
profile and label the calibration state accurately.
